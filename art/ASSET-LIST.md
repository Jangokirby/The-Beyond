# The Beyond: picture list for an illustrated edition

523 images. Every row in `asset-list.csv` is one picture, with its file name, size and a description to turn into a prompt.

## Version 2: what changed

- **Places in the written cases are described by what they look like**, not by what happens there (tools/scene-prompts.js). The first pictures were painted from story text, so the generator made ordinary rooms and fields: the crater chapel got a roof, the trench had no trench. Every case place and fight backdrop is marked **[redo]**.
- **A negative column.** Each row lists what must stay out of that picture (people, a roof, a cabin...). Add it to your usual negative prompt.
- **Items are new** (**[new]**): gear and the things you click in scenes (periscope, telephone, letter...). Until they exist the game shows drawn icons.
- `asset-list-v2-todo.csv` holds only the [redo] and [new] rows: that is the batch to run. Same file names as before, so the new pictures drop straight in.

## How to make them

- **One style for everything.** Decide the style once (for example: painted, ink-and-wash, muted colour, film grain) and put the same style words at the start of every prompt. The descriptions below only say what's in each picture.
- **No text, logos, watermarks, signatures or frames** in any picture.
- **Sizes:** portraits and monsters are square (1024x1024), places are wide (1344x768). PNG, JPG or WEBP are all fine; keep each file under about 1 MB if you can.
- **Portraits:** head and shoulders, the face in the upper middle, plain dark background. They are shown in circles and small cards, so faces must read at small size.
- **Monsters:** full figure, centred, with space around it; they appear as round tokens in fights.
- **Places:** wide establishing views without people in the foreground. The right third of the picture is often covered by character portraits.
- **File names matter.** Save each image with exactly the file name in the list, keeping the folders (for example `portraits/agents/victorian-f-01.png`). Then zip the whole folder and send it over; I'll wire everything in as its own edition, next to the drawn and photo editions.
- Anything you skip falls back to the drawn art, so you can deliver in batches. A good first batch: the agent portraits for one era, the characters and places of one case, and a few monsters.
