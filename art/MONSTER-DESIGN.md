# How monsters look in The Beyond

From the rulebook: *"The Beyond is a dimension that warps reality... capturing our deepest fears and making them real...
allowing manifestations from any era to appear in our reality."* Its gods are *"consciousness, twisted and given life...
a foundation in human thought."*

So a monster is never just a creature. It is **the Beyond's copy of something people know or imagine, made wrong.**
Underneath each one is a human fear or desire. The era decides what it copies: the thing people of that time
lived with, worshipped, or were afraid of.

## Not everything is a creature

The book's own types say so: a Construct is *"the chairs, doors, tables, and buildings"*, and Beyond Artifacts are
cursed things that once belonged to someone. A case's threat can be:

- **A place**: a house that wasn't there last year, a room that copies rooms from your past, a street stuck in one hour.
  Picture it as architecture that has become a body: doorways for mouths, wallpaper that breathes, windows for eyes.
- **A cursed object**: an heirloom, a music box, a portrait, a reel of film, a séance board. Picture the object itself,
  centred, with one thing about it alive or human that shouldn't be.
- **An event**: a storm, a repeating hour, a broadcast. Picture its shape as a figure: the dust wall with a man's goggles,
  the clock with too many hands.

In the game these can often be ended with a rite instead of a fight (side jobs: "Unmake the house", "Break the
curse", "Break the loop"; written cases: Vacancy, The Sitting Room, The Last Picture Show, Quiet Hours).

## The recipe for every monster prompt

1. **What it copies.** A real, recognisable thing from its time: a saint's statue, a photograph, a telegram, a TV advert, a
   mascot, a phone screen. Or a thing people imagined: a folk tale, a ghost story, a film monster.
2. **What's wrong with it.** The Beyond gets the copy almost right, then breaks it: too many of something, the wrong
   material, a part that's alive that shouldn't be, a face where no face belongs, scale that's off.
3. **The fear underneath.** Grief, being forgotten, being watched, hunger, judgement, conformity, loneliness. Pick one;
   it decides the mood and the "wrong" detail.
4. **Era flavour.** Medieval leans folklore and church; Victorian leans mourning, photography, gaslight, mechanism;
   the Wars lean mud, paper, gas, telegrams; Atomic leans adverts, suburbia, radiation, TV; Late Century leans pop
   culture, video tapes, mascots, urban legends; Present leans screens, feeds, smart devices, a touch of cyberpunk.
   Any era can borrow from another: the Beyond is outside time.
5. **Framing.** Full figure, centred, filling most of the square, plain dark background, nothing else in the picture.
   It's shown as a round token in fights.

## Do and don't

- **Don't** draw a person in a costume, a mask or a uniform. A gas mask on a soldier is a soldier. The *helmet full of
  barbed wire with a mother's voice coming out of it* is a monster.
- **Don't** draw the real thing it copies. A drone is a drone. A drone that has grown a ribcage and hums a lullaby is ours.
- **Don't** copy a specific film, game or brand character. Draw the *idea* people are afraid of (a cursed tape,
  a theme-park mascot, a sleep-paralysis visitor), not someone's design.
- **Do** keep something recognisable, so players know what it's a copy of in one look.
- **Do** give it one human detail in the wrong place: hands, teeth, a wedding ring, a child's shoe.

## Negative prompt for every monster

`(ordinary person, man in a mask, costume, cosplay, soldier in uniform, realistic animal, real machine, vehicle:1.3),
(cute, friendly, cartoon:1.3), (several figures, crowd, people in the background:1.3), cropped, text, watermark, frame`
